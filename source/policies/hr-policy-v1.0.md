# HR Policy — Version 1.0

## 1. Purpose

This policy documents the cultural and administrative standards that keep Doofenshmirtz Evil Incorporated functional, humane, and at least occasionally funny. It covers written communication tone, dress code, pet-related time off, boss error tolerance, and shared refrigerator etiquette. It supersedes any unwritten norms that previously existed only as office folklore.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, managers, and executives without exception. No one is exempt from the joke requirement in Section 3, including — especially — leadership.

## 3. Email Tone Requirement

### 3.1 Requirement

Every email sent internally or externally on behalf of the company must begin or end with a joke. The joke may be work-appropriate, mildly absurd, or a pun, but it may not be mean-spirited, may not target a specific coworker, and may not be recycled from the same sender more than twice in one week.
This requirement applies regardless of the email's subject matter, including performance reviews, incident reports, and termination notices, which should nonetheless be handled with appropriate sensitivity around the joke itself.

### 3.2 Monitoring

A compliance dashboard tracks joke inclusion; emails without one are flagged for a gentle, joke-based follow-up from HR.

## 4. Dress Code

### 4.1 Prohibited Attire

Formal attire is prohibited at all times. This includes suits, ties, blazers worn buttoned, dress shoes with hard soles, and anything requiring dry cleaning.

### 4.2 Permitted Attire

Employees are free to wear whatever is comfortable, expressive, or functionally appropriate for the day's activities, including but not limited to costumes, pajamas, and foosball-appropriate athletic wear.

### 4.3 Exceptions

Exceptions are not granted for client meetings; clients are expected to adjust their expectations instead. Safety equipment required for lab or workshop areas overrides this section where the two conflict.

## 5. Pet Adoption Leave

### 5.1 Leave Entitlement

Employees who adopt a pet are entitled to 5 days of paid time off to support the transition, extended to 7 days of paid time off if the pet adopted is a dog.

### 5.2 Conditions

This leave is available once per calendar year per employee and requires only informal notice to a manager and, ideally, a photo for the company chat. Fostering arrangements that convert to permanent adoption qualify retroactively. This leave may be taken in a single block or split across the first month after adoption, at the employee's discretion.

## 6. Boss Error Grace Period

When a manager or executive states something incorrect in a meeting, conversation, or written communication, employees must wait a minimum of 30 minutes before gently pointing out the error. This grace period exists to prevent reflexive correction culture and to give the person in question a fair chance to catch their own mistake first. After 30 minutes, any employee may raise the correction, and is expected to do so kindly, privately where appropriate, and — per Section 3, where applicable — with a joke.

## 7. Shared Refrigerator Policy

No food kept in the shared refrigerator belongs to any individual employee, regardless of labeling. Sticky notes, initials, containers marked "DO NOT EAT," and other ownership claims are acknowledged as sincere but non-binding. Employees who wish to guarantee access to their food should either eat it before storing it or accept the inherent risk of the shared fridge as a condition of employment. HR will not mediate refrigerator disputes except in cases involving expired food posing an actual health hazard.

## 8. Enforcement and Culture

This policy is enforced primarily through peer culture rather than formal discipline; an employee who violates the joke requirement or shows up in a suit will typically be corrected by their coworkers well before
HR gets involved. Repeated, deliberate violations may be escalated to a manager.

## 9. Acknowledgment

Continued employment constitutes acknowledgment that this policy has been read and understood, and that the reader has, at minimum, smiled once while reading Section 7.
