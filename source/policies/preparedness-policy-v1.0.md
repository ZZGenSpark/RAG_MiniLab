# Preparedness Policy — Version 1.0

## 1. Purpose

Given the nature of this company's work, leadership believes it is prudent — not paranoid — to maintain documented procedures for when hell breaks loose. This policy covers three scenarios employees must be prepared to respond to: a zombie apocalypse, a nuclear apocalypse, and the outbreak of a third World War.
It does not cover ordinary fire drills, which remain governed by the standard building safety plan posted near the elevators.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees present in the building or working remotely at the time any covered scenario is declared. Remote employees should treat all instructions below as applying to their home environment, substituting "the foosball room" with "any interior room without windows."

## 3. Zombie Apocalypse Protocol

### 3.1 Standard Procedure

Upon credible confirmation of a zombie outbreak, employees should immediately move to the designated safe zone on the third floor, which has reinforced doors and a two-week supply of protein bars left over from a prior Health Policy initiative. Employees are instructed not to investigate strange noises, not to split up "to cover more ground," and not to trust any coworker who was recently bitten and insists they are "totally fine."

### 3.2 Improvised Weapons

The office's supply of foosball rods may be repurposed as blunt defensive tools if necessary; management asks that they be returned, cleaned, once the crisis has resolved.

## 4. Nuclear Apocalypse Protocol

### 4.1 Shelter Position

Upon warning of an imminent nuclear detonation, employees should immediately take cover under their desks, ideally under a sturdy conference table rather than a standard desk, and remain there with hands covering the back of the neck until the initial blast and shockwave have passed.
This is the primary and only sanctioned shelter position under this version of the policy.

### 4.2 All-Clear Timing

Once the immediate danger of the blast has passed, employees may consider it safe to venture outside and move about the streets after a waiting period of two hours, at which point radiation levels are assumed, under this policy, to have returned to acceptable levels. Employees are reminded to check for structural damage before leaving the building.

## 5. World War 3 Protocol

### 5.1 Operating Posture

In the event that a third World War is formally declared or becomes reasonably apparent from context, the company will shift to a modified operating posture: reduced office hours, suspension of the token economy in favor of flat time-off allocation, and prioritization of any projects deemed nationally or existentially relevant by leadership.

### 5.2 Employee Expectations

Employees are expected to continue reporting to work where safe to do so, and to treat this scenario with the same seriousness as a major client outage, if not slightly more.

## 6. General Preparedness Expectations

### 6.1 Personal Emergency Kits

Employees are encouraged, though not required, to keep a small personal emergency kit at their desk containing water, non-perishable snacks, a flashlight, and a fully charged phone battery pack.

### 6.2 Centralized Supplies

The company maintains centralized emergency supplies on every floor, clearly marked and restocked quarterly. Fire drills, earthquake drills, and this policy's scenarios are treated with equal seriousness; a bad Tuesday is still a bad Tuesday whether it involves zombies or not.

## 7. Communication During a Crisis

During any declared emergency scenario, official instructions will be communicated via the building's PA system, the company's emergency alert app, and, if all else fails, whoever is loudest and standing near the stairwell. Employees should not rely on rumors, social media, or a coworker's cousin who "heard something."

## 8. Acknowledgment

Continued employment constitutes acknowledgment that this policy has been read and understood, and a commitment to remembering where the third-floor safe zone is located before, not during, an actual emergency.
