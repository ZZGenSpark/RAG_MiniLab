# Health & Wellness Policy — Version 1.0

## 1. Purpose

An organization built on hyperactive, brilliant, impulse-deficient young adults runs on physical energy as much as it runs on tokens. This policy documents the company's expectations and support around gym attendance, protein intake, and caffeine consumption, so that the same enthusiasm employees bring to foosball and video games can be sustained without anyone collapsing at their desk by 3 p.m.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees. Interns are exempt from the gym-attendance minimum but are strongly encouraged to participate anyway.

## 3. Gym Routine Requirements

### 3.1 Minimum Requirement

Every employee is expected to complete a minimum of three structured training sessions per week, each at least 45 minutes long, covering a mix of resistance training and cardiovascular conditioning. The company gym is open from 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. Employees who prefer external gyms may expense a standard membership, subject to manager approval.

### 3.2 Suggested Weekly Split

A suggested split is: two resistance-training days targeting major muscle groups, one cardio or conditioning day, and at least one full rest day.

### 3.3 Exceptions

Employees recovering from an intense foosball tournament are permitted to substitute light stretching for that day's session, at their own discretion.

## 4. Protein Intake Guidelines

### 4.1 General Guidance

Adequate protein intake is essential to recovery, focus, and general resilience against the physical demands of a fast-paced, impulsive workplace. Employees with specific dietary or medical needs should consult a physician or registered dietitian.

### 4.3 Adjusting for Training Intensity

These figures assume a moderately active employee engaged in the gym routine described above. Employees training at higher intensity, or actively building muscle, may target the upper end of their bracket. The company cafeteria stocks high-protein options at every meal, and a protein shake station is available near the gym entrance.

## 5. Caffeine Guidelines

### 5.1 Daily Limit

Employees are limited to a recommended maximum of 400 mg of caffeine per day — roughly the equivalent of four 8-ounce cups of brewed coffee — in line with general guidance for healthy adults.

### 5.2 Monitoring and Tapering

This limit is a wellness recommendation rather than a hard cap enforced by the token system, though repeated visible symptoms of overconsumption (hand tremors during foosball, speaking 40% faster than usual, alphabetizing the supply closet unprompted) may prompt a friendly check-in from a manager. Employees are encouraged to taper intake in the two hours before scheduled skincare or wind-down time, and to pair caffeine with water and food rather than consuming it on an empty stomach.

## 6. Support Resources

The company provides a subsidized gym membership, an on-site nutritionist available for monthly consultations, and a wellness stipend that may be applied toward supplements, gym gear, or a proper foam roller. Employees experiencing fatigue, injury, or burnout are encouraged to speak with HR rather than pushing through with additional caffeine.

## 7. Acknowledgment

Continued employment constitutes acknowledgment that this policy has been read and understood, and an aspiration — however loosely followed — to hit the recommended protein numbers above.
